# 2026-07-28: The unexplained-tone sweep: real gain, wrong bucket

**Goal.** Run the sweep pre-declared in log -04: unexplainedToneCost at
2.0/1.5/1.0/0.75/0.5 on arms A0 and BC-modal, development split, read against
the declared expectations.

**What happened.** Mean per piece, rescued 23-movement roster:

| utc  | A0 exact | A0 root | BC exact | BC root |
| ---- | -------- | ------- | -------- | ------- |
| 2.0  | 0.602    | 0.747   | 0.574    | 0.736   |
| 1.5  | 0.602    | 0.747   | 0.575    | 0.736   |
| 1.0  | 0.606    | 0.752   | 0.583    | 0.745   |
| 0.75 | 0.606    | 0.753   | 0.583    | 0.745   |
| 0.5  | 0.595    | 0.754   | 0.572    | 0.746   |

A plateau at 1.0-0.75, then the pre-declared laziness signature exactly on
schedule at 0.5: exact drops below baseline while root keeps rising, subset
names gaining roots and losing families. At utc 1.0 the paired gains are real:
A0 +0.0042, CI95 [+0.0013, +0.0074] (11/4/8); BC +0.0091, CI95 [+0.0038,
+0.0160] (15/2/6).

**But the gain lands in the wrong bucket.** The pre-declaration expected the
added-tone family to move first, melody-absorption samples flipping. They do
not: the census shows the recovered time comes from the partial bucket
(incomplete voicings now named as base chords the analyst agrees with), while
playable/appSuperset barely moves. The flagship melody case (D minor plus a held
C# melody) never flips at any swept price, and the arithmetic forbids it:
Dm(maj7) explains everything at cost 0.1, so the honest Dm reading cannot win
until the unexplained price falls below 0.1, far past the laziness collapse. The
absorption bucket's real lever is the rarity price of the absorbing vocabulary
(minorMajor7 at 0.1 is what makes the melody rename cheap), not the
unexplained-tone price.

**Guards at utc 1.0:** standing rows zero flips at every price (their canonical
cases all have full-explanation readings, so the lever never enters their
competition; no review-on-flip obligations). Dense census (classical, thin)
unchanged at 1.0000 consistency; the POP909 dense guard and pool blast radius
are deferred to any adoption proposal, per protocol.

**Verdict.** The lever works modestly and safely in 1.5 to 0.75, but the
adoption bar requires the change to move the bucket it claims, and this one
moves a different bucket than declared. Not proposed for adoption as-is. Two
follow-ups recorded:

1. If utc near 1.0 is pursued, it re-enters with a revised claim
   (partial-content base naming) and its own pre-declaration.
2. The absorption bucket's lever is vocabulary rarity pricing: scope what
   minorMajor7 and its rare-color kin cost today, what legitimate usage they
   carry (exposure weights), and what raising them would flip. That scoping is
   the natural next entry.

**Plain-English reading.** We made unexplained notes cheaper to forgive, and the
engine did get a little better, but not at the thing we were aiming at. The
improvement came from half-played chords finally getting their simple names; the
melody-swallowing habit never budged, because the fancy name it reaches for is
so cheap that no discount on honesty can compete. Push the discount far enough
to matter and the engine turns lazy, slapping simple names on everything. So the
dial stays where it was, and the chase moves to the actual culprit: how little
the fancy vocabulary costs.
