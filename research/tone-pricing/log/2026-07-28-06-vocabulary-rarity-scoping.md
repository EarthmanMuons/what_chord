# 2026-07-28: Vocabulary-rarity scoping: the melody bucket is not honestly priceable

**Goal.** Scope the vocabulary-rarity lever the utc sweep redirected to (log
-05): what the absorbing vocabulary costs, what legitimate usage it carries, and
what raising it could flip.

**What happened.**

**Absorption by absorbing-quality tier** (A0 dev, 275 s of added-tone time):
68.3% flows through common-tier vocabulary at price 0 (major7 20.8%, dominant7
19.6%, minor7 12.7%, minor6 11.2%), 21.5% through marked (0.1), 10.1% uncommon
(0.4), 0.1% rare (1.0). The common-tier majority is largely defensible surface
naming: the app hearing C-E-G-B and saying Cmaj7 while the analyst writes I is
naming a genuinely sounding seventh, the ruler culture difference again, not an
engine defect. Raising common-tier prices is untouchable regardless.

**The lopsided absorbers.** minorMajor7 carries 13.7% of absorption with exactly
zero seconds of analyst-agreeing usage anywhere in the development split;
major7sus4 is 7.5% absorbing against 8 s legitimate. These are the weird-name
generators.

**Frequency evidence** (ChoCo observations of 1.1M): minorMajor7 sits at 1,365,
the rarest member of its marked tier (halfDiminished7 14,746, diminished7 8,083,
augmented 4,760, dominant7sus4 4,179, sus2 1,423) and below two uncommon-tier
members (dominant7Sharp5 7,470, dominant7Flat5 2,426). A promotion to uncommon
(0.1 to 0.4) is frequency-justified on its own, independent of the absorption
argument. POP909 shows m(maj7) at 0.44% of top-1 event mass, real but thin, and
some of that is likely absorption too.

**The arithmetic that closes the question.** The flagship melody case flips only
when the m(maj7) price meets or exceeds the unexplained-tone price. At
frequency-honest prices (uncommon 0.4) against any sane utc (1.0 or above, per
the sweep's laziness floor at 0.5), the spread never closes: 0.4 versus 1.0
still names the melody into the chord. Pricing m(maj7) at rare (1.0) exceeds its
frequency justification (it is three times more common than that tier's spirit),
and utc below 0.75 regresses. **The melody-absorption bucket is not reachable by
any frequency-honest pricing**; its true levers remain structural (voice
separation, shelved with its ceiling) or display-side.

**What survives: two modest, independently defensible candidates.**

1. minorMajor7 tier correction, marked to uncommon: justified purely by
   frequency (rarer than existing uncommon members), shaves the cheapest
   absorption at the margins, and honestly prices a name the corpus says is
   rarer than its tier-mates.
2. utc at 1.0 with the revised claim from log -05 (partial-content base naming,
   +0.4 to +0.9 points, CI95 excluding zero).

Both are small; a combined pre-declared experiment (tier correction plus utc
1.0) with full guards (pool blast radius exposure-weighted, standing rows, dense
census including the POP909 side, solo goldens, comping) is the natural single
package if pursued.

**Plain-English reading.** The fancy-name discount turns out to be mostly
honest: two thirds of the swallowed extra notes get swallowed into everyday
names that genuinely fit what was sounding. The one name that is almost pure
artifact on this corpus, minor-major seventh, is also priced a tier cheaper than
its real-world rarity deserves, so correcting that tier is worth doing on its
own merits. But no honest price ever makes the engine un-hear a melody note that
truly sounds inside the chord; that fix belongs to a future that can tell melody
from harmony, which we have already priced and shelved.

**Next.** Decision point: run the combined modest package (tier correction plus
utc 1.0) under a fresh pre-declaration, or record the superset side as resolved
and move to the shell lever design (power gate relaxation plus seventh-as-color
vocabulary, blockers mapped in log -04).
