# 2026-07-28: POP909 diligence: admitted for stability and voicings, weak oracle for identity

**Goal.** Execute avenue 4: license and annotation-quality diligence on POP909
(909 pop-song piano arrangements, ISMIR 2020) before any commitment.

**Setup.** Sources: the dataset repository (music-x-lab/POP909-Dataset, cloned
at depth 1 to `build/whatkey-corpora/POP909-Dataset`, 145 MB), its LICENSE and
README, and the paper (arXiv:2008.07142). Local probes on samples: MIDI
character (pedal, velocity, timing) on 5 songs; chord-label content agreement on
31 songs spread across the corpus, using the census-style method (time-weighted
overlap between label chord tones and sounding pitch classes, with a
whole-second shift response as the alignment sanity check); scratchpad probe,
method recorded here.

**What happened.**

License: the repository is MIT (the authors' arrangements and annotations); the
paper is CC BY 4.0. Neither discusses the underlying compositions: 909 popular
songs by 462 artists, plainly copyrighted. Verdict: same handling as ASAP
regardless of the nominally permissive repo license, fixtures stay under
`build/`, extractors refuse `research/` writes, only splits, manifests, and
hashes get committed.

Input character: genuinely performance-flavored. Sustain pedal CC64 present (970
events across 5 sampled songs), expressive velocities on nearly every note,
inter-onset gaps forming a 2-40 ms continuum rather than a grid, and
hand-labeled tempo curves aligning the MIDI to the original audio. Three tracks
per song (MELODY, BRIDGE, PIANO) which merge naturally in replay. The existing
pedal-aware `sounding_snapshots` consumes this as-is.

Annotation provenance, the paper's answer: chords and keys are machine-extracted
(audio chords via a large-vocabulary transcription algorithm snapped to beats;
MIDI chords via a beat-segmented template algorithm; keys via an audio
algorithm), with cross-agreement as the only quality control (root match above
75% for 800+ songs, a few below 40%). Not analyst ground truth.

Annotation quality, measured (31 songs, 7,600+ labeled seconds):

| labels      | source         | overlap at 0 | at +-1 s    | root sounding |
| ----------- | -------------- | ------------ | ----------- | ------------- |
| chord_midi  | from the MIDI  | 0.916        | 0.71 / 0.66 | 0.990         |
| chord_audio | from the audio | 0.884        | 0.71 / 0.66 | 0.983         |

Both label streams peak sharply at zero shift. The chord_midi agreement is
partly circular (extracted from the same notes), so the chord_audio row is the
meaningful one: labels derived independently from the original recordings agree
with the arrangement content at 0.88, which simultaneously validates the labels'
alignment and the arrangements' harmonic fidelity.

**Fitness verdict, per use:**

- **Stability ruler: admitted, and the best immediate use.** The stability
  metrics need no ground truth at all, and POP909 is exactly the non-classical
  performed corpus the corpus-breadth concern called for. Replicating the
  display-policy frontier here is pure plumbing (a small extractor reusing the
  ASAP snapshot and replay path).
- **Observed-voicing sampling (avenue 3): admitted.** Professional pop
  arrangements with expressive voicings, exactly the doubling and omission
  patterns the oracle pool lacks.
- **Identity ruler: weak advisory oracle only.** The chord labels are another
  algorithm's output, beat-quantized and unverified by humans. Good enough (0.88
  independent content agreement) to rank disagreements worth eyeballing in the
  pop idiom; not good enough to headline an accuracy number against. Any
  identity-style use gets labeled advisory, like the oracle-comparison
  libraries.
- **Key labels: not needed** (whatkey has stronger key corpora).

**Plain-English reading.** The pop dataset checks out better than expected: the
piano playing is real playing, pedal and all, and its machine-made chord labels
agree with what is actually sounding about nine tenths of the time, which is
plenty to steer by and not enough to grade against. The copyright situation of
the underlying songs means we keep everything derived from it out of the
repository, same as the classical corpus. The first payoff is the one the review
asked for: re-running the flicker numbers on music that is not Beethoven.

**Decisions.**

- POP909 admitted under ASAP-style gating for the stability ruler and
  observed-voicing sampling; identity use is advisory-only. README avenue 4
  gains the verdict.
- No split needed yet (stability replication is descriptive, no tuning); if
  identity-adjacent tuning ever uses POP909, a split freezes first, per
  protocol.

**Next.** Build the POP909 extraction path (snapshot replay with frames, reusing
the ASAP tooling) and replicate the stability baseline and display-policy
frontier on the pop corpus, closing the loop on the corpus-breadth caveat in log
-15.
